# Revision 2: minimal and abstract

## User feedback

The initial three boards were rejected as too detailed and unlike On's minimal, abstract iconography. String construction, gauge and maximum tension were the strongest subjects. Those concepts remain the starting points; the rest need much less visual information.

This feedback supersedes the initial recommendation to use whole-object illustrations as the family baseline. Do not restore detailed shoes, bags, rackets, tread, lacing, stitching or fabric mesh in future iterations.

## Revised drawing grammar

- Show the minimum shape needed to identify an attribute with its label.
- Prefer partial contours, simple sections, curves, circles and a few measurement marks.
- Balance: beam and pivot. Flex: curve and neutral reference. Ventilation: three airflow lines. Compartments: a divided open outline.
- Retain the gauge cross-section, filament circles and sparse string-bed tension idea.
- Use thin black linework, generous empty space and no decorative detail. The brief targets 2–5 primary strokes for most icons; tension and construction may need more.
- Icons are attribute signs. They do not assert actual construction, performance, measurements or compartment counts for every product.

## Visual review

- All twelve subjects and labels appear on each new board. Whole rackets, full detailed shoes, bags, dense string grids and tread textures have been removed.
- Gauge and string construction remain distinct circular sections. Max tension retains a sparse string grid; shaft flex uses a separate curved-line diagram.
- The first board's tension arrows point inward. Use the outward-facing arrows in the second and third boards when refining the tension concept.
- The third board's shaft reference uses dimension-like end ticks. A plain or dashed neutral line would communicate bending more clearly.
- The support arch is intentionally abstract, but can suggest an anatomical arch; validate its meaning with the eventual product label before adoption. The feather may also read as a leaf without its Weight label.
- The boards reduce detail substantially, but visual acceptance remains with the user. No claim is made that they are already a precise match to On or production-ready.
- These are raster explorations generated using the built-in Image Generation tool. Exact prompts and attached-reference paths are recorded in `revision-2-prompts.json`. Pixel-size icon checks and final SVG geometry remain pending.
